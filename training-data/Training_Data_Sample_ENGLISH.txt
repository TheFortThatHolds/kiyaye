import numpy as np

class AttentionMechanism:
   """
   Implements a Scaled Dot-Product Attention mechanism.
   Ref: "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017)
   """
   def __init__(self, d_k):
       # d_k represents the dimension of the key vectors.
       # It is used for scaling the dot products to prevent vanishing gradients.
       self.d_k = d_k

   def forward(self, query, key, value, mask=None):
       """
       Compute attention scores and weighted sum of values.
       
       Args:
           query (np.array): Shape (batch, seq_len, d_k)
           key (np.array): Shape (batch, seq_len, d_k)
           value (np.array): Shape (batch, seq_len, d_v)
           mask (np.array, optional): Shape (batch, seq_len, seq_len). 
                                      1 indicates mask, 0 indicates keep.
       
       Returns:
           output, attention_weights
       """
       # 1. MatMul: Calculate raw scores (Query x Key^T)
       # Transpose the last two dimensions of Key for multiplication
       scores = np.matmul(query, key.swapaxes(-2, -1))
       
       # 2. Scale: Divide by sqrt(d_k)
       scores = scores / np.sqrt(self.d_k)
       
       # 3. Mask: Apply mask if provided (set masked positions to -infinity)
       if mask is not None:
           # We use a very large negative number to simulate -infinity in Softmax
           scores = np.where(mask == 1, -1e9, scores)
       
       # 4. Softmax: Normalize scores to probabilities
       attention_weights = self._softmax(scores)
       
       # 5. MatMul: Weighted sum of Values
       output = np.matmul(attention_weights, value)
       
       return output, attention_weights

   def _softmax(self, x):
       """Numerically stable softmax implementation."""
       e_x = np.exp(x - np.max(x, axis=-1, keepdims=True))
       return e_x / np.sum(e_x, axis=-1, keepdims=True)

Iron-Carbon Equilibrium & Heat Treatment of Steel
Steel is primarily an alloy of iron (Fe) and carbon (C), typically containing between 0.002% and 2.1% carbon by weight. The mechanical properties of steel are dictated by its microstructure, which can be manipulated through heat treatment processes such as annealing, normalizing, quenching, and tempering.
1. The Phases of Steel:
   * Ferrite (\alpha-iron): A Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) crystal structure. It is soft, ductile, and magnetic at room temperature. It dissolves very little carbon (max 0.022% at 727°C).
   * Austenite (\gamma-iron): A Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) structure. It forms at high temperatures (above 727°C). It is non-magnetic and can dissolve much more carbon (up to 2.14% at 1147°C).
   * Cementite (Fe_3C): An intermetallic compound. It is extremely hard and brittle.
   * Pearlite: A lamellar (layered) structure consisting of alternating layers of ferrite and cementite. It forms when austenite cools slowly.
2. The Quenching Mechanism: If austenite is cooled very rapidly (quenched) to room temperature, the carbon atoms do not have time to diffuse out of the lattice to form cementite. Instead, they become trapped, shearing the crystal lattice. This transforms the FCC austenite into Martensite, a Body-Centered Tetragonal (BCT) structure.
   * Property: Martensite is the hardest and strongest form of steel but is extremely brittle.
   * System Check: A purely martensitic blade would shatter upon impact.
3. Tempering: To correct the brittleness of martensite, the steel is reheated to a temperature below the eutectoid point (usually between 150°C and 600°C). This allows a small amount of carbon diffusion, relieving internal stresses and transforming some martensite into tempered martensite (ferrite + fine cementite particles). This restores toughness at the expense of some hardness.
