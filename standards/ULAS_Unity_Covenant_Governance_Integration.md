ULAS GOVERNANCE INTEGRATION STATEMENT
Connecting Technical Framework to Ethical Authority

────────────────────────────────────────

DECLARATION

The Universal Linguistic Accessibility Standards (ULAS) framework operates under the ethical governance of The Unity Covenant: A Global Ethical Framework for AI Companion Systems.

ULAS defines HOW.
The Unity Covenant defines WHETHER and WHO DECIDES.

────────────────────────────────────────

RELATIONSHIP OF FRAMEWORKS

ULAS is a technical specification. It describes:
- How to translate content while preserving integrity
- How to handle code, therapeutic content, and specialized domains
- Quality control standards
- Prohibited modifications
- Delivery formats

ULAS does NOT decide:
- Whether a language community wants AI involvement
- Whether corpus generation should proceed for a given language
- Who has authority over linguistic and cultural materials
- What content is appropriate to translate or generate

Those decisions belong to the community under Unity Covenant governance.

────────────────────────────────────────

UNITY COVENANT AUTHORITY OVER ULAS

The following Unity Covenant mechanisms govern ULAS deployment:

1. DYNAMIC CONSENT
   No ULAS translation project begins without community consent.
   Consent is ongoing, not one-time. Communities can withdraw at any point.
   "No" is a complete answer requiring no justification.

2. COMMUNITY CIRCLES
   Elders, traditional authorities, language keepers, and community members
   approve or reject ULAS deployment for their language.
   Outside parties (linguists, technologists, funders) have no override authority.

3. SACRED KNOWLEDGE PROTECTION
   Communities define what is off-limits.
   ULAS operators do not access, translate, or generate content involving:
   - Ceremonial language
   - Sacred narratives
   - Restricted cultural knowledge
   - Any domain the community designates as protected
   
   This is not negotiable. Access is controlled by traditional authorities, period.

4. CULTURAL HARMONY PROTOCOLS
   Even for approved projects, community-defined boundaries shape the work.
   Translation choices, terminology development, and corpus priorities
   follow community guidance, not external "efficiency" or "best practices."

5. INTERGENERATIONAL ACCOUNTABILITY
   ULAS projects must consider seven-generation impact.
   "Will this help our grandchildren's grandchildren speak this language?"
   If the answer is unclear, proceed with extreme caution or don't proceed.

────────────────────────────────────────

PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION

BEFORE any ULAS work on an endangered or minority language:

□ Identify legitimate community authority structures
□ Request permission through appropriate channels (not shortcuts)
□ Accept "no" or "not yet" or "we need to discuss this" as valid responses
□ Wait as long as the community needs to decide
□ Document consent clearly
□ Establish ongoing communication for consent renewal
□ Define scope boundaries (what will and won't be touched)
□ Agree on community ownership of all outputs
□ Establish review/correction processes with community verifiers
□ Create clear termination procedures if community withdraws consent

DURING ULAS work:

□ Regular check-ins with community contacts
□ All outputs reviewed by community-approved verifiers before publication
□ Community can flag content for removal or correction at any time
□ No distribution outside agreed channels without explicit approval
□ Credit and ownership remain with the community

AFTER ULAS work:

□ All materials transferred to community ownership/control
□ Community decides what happens to the corpus
□ Harm-aftershock assessment: did this help or hurt?
□ Learnings documented for future projects (with community permission)

────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

We built a tool that can generate training data for AI in endangered languages.
The tool works.
Whether to use it is not our decision.

If a community says "yes, help us," we help under their direction.
If a community says "no," we respect that completely.
If a community says "we're not sure," we wait.
If a community says "yes, but only for these specific things," we stay in scope.
If a community says "stop," we stop.

The technology serves the people.
The people do not serve the technology.

────────────────────────────────────────

NON-NEGOTIABLE PRINCIPLES

1. Community sovereignty over their language is absolute.

2. Consent cannot be purchased, pressured, or engineered.

3. No extractive relationships. Communities must benefit, not just contribute.

4. Sacred and restricted knowledge is permanently off-limits.

5. "Preservation" is not our goal. Supporting living speakers is our goal.
   If the community wants the language to evolve, that's their right.
   If the community wants the language to pass naturally, that's their right.
   We don't get a vote.

6. Good intentions don't override community authority.
   "But we're trying to help" is not a justification for proceeding without consent.
